Privacy Policy
eSportly

Last updated: November 10, 2026 · Version française · Versión en español · Deutsche Fassung · Versão em português

eSportly is an iPhone and iPad app that lets you follow esports teams and players and get an alert before their matches. This page explains, in plain language, what data the app uses.

In short: there is no account, your favorites stay on your device, and the app never gives us your name or email address. If you turn on alerts, our server keeps only what it needs to send them.

1. Who is responsible

ProtoJo Digital (Johan Quille, sole proprietorship), France. Contact: contact@protojo.fr. Full details are in the legal notice on protojo.fr (in French).

2. What stays on your device

This information is not sent to us and is removed when you delete the app:

  • your favorite teams, players and competitions;
  • your settings (alerts, quiet hours, hidden scores);
  • the latest matches loaded, and the logos and photos already shown, for offline display.

The app does not access your contacts, photos, location, microphone or camera. “Add to calendar” opens the iOS event sheet: you confirm the event yourself, and the app never reads your calendar.

Friend challenge. If you pick a winner for a match, your pick stays on your phone (and in your iCloud, if you use it). When you share a challenge, the link only contains the match, the chosen team and a random code specific to that match, which says nothing about you. Picks received from your friends are kept on your phone for 30 days, then deleted. We receive none of this information. There is no money and no betting.

Apple Intelligence. The match summary and the “While you were away” recap are written by Apple Intelligence directly on your iPhone, from match results: nothing is sent to us or to any other service. These texts are labelled as generated, and you can turn them off in the app's settings.

3. What our server keeps if you turn on alerts

Alerts are sent by our server. For that purpose, and only if you allowed notifications and follow at least one team or competition, it stores:

DataWhat it is for
A random identifier generated by the appRecognizing your device without knowing who you are
The notification token provided by AppleDelivering the alert to you
The app language and your time zoneWriting the alert in the right language and respecting your quiet hours
Your alert settings and quiet hoursSending only the alerts you want
The teams, players and competitions you follow, and matches you mutedKnowing which matches to alert you about
The token provided by Apple that lets us start the Lock Screen score, if you turned it onShowing the score on your Lock Screen as soon as a match starts
The update token of a score shown on your Lock Screen, for the duration of a matchUpdating that score after each game
A random token, if you subscribe to the calendar of your matchesServing that calendar to your Calendar app without knowing who you are

Legal basis: performance of the service you request (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)).

Retention: everything is deleted as soon as you no longer follow anything or turn off all alerts, when Apple tells us the token is no longer valid (for example after you delete the app), and at the latest after 90 days without hearing from your device. The update token of a score is deleted when the match ends; the token that starts the display is kept while alerts are on.

The server also keeps counters with no personal data (number of registered devices, alerts sent per day, calls received from the genuine app or not) to monitor that the service works properly.

App verification (Apple App Attest): to protect the service against copies, about once a month the app proves to our server that it is the genuine eSportly app installed on a real device. To do so it creates a random key, which we keep with its identifier for 60 days at most, with nothing that could identify you. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in securing the service (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

3a. The "Who will win?" poll

When you answer the "Who will win?" poll on a match page, the app sends to our server the randomly generated identifier (the same as for alerts), the match, and your choice (left or right team). We do not know who you are. The server adds up everyone's votes to show percentages; your choice is not linked to your identity. Legal basis: performance of the service you requested (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)). Retention: your vote is deleted at most 30 days after you vote, or as soon as you choose "Erase everything" in the privacy settings.

3b. Usage statistics

To understand how the app is used and improve it, the app sends our server a few usage statistics: the screens you open and how long you stay there, opening the subscription page, searches, the start, completion or failure of an eSportly+ purchase, restoring a purchase, and opening an alert. Each batch also carries the app and OS version and your country (two letters). These statistics are tied to a randomly generated device identifier created at first launch — not the advertising identifier, nor your Apple account identifier — and we do not know your name or email address. They are never combined with data from other apps or websites and are never sold.

You can turn them off at any time: in Settings, switch off "Share usage statistics". The app then immediately stops sending them and clears those still waiting. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in measuring and improving the service (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). Retention: statistics are kept on our server for 25 months at most.

4. Services the app relies on

  • Supabase: hosts our server, in the European Union (Ireland). It keeps technical logs, which may contain your device's IP address, to keep the service secure. These logs are kept for 7 days at most. We do not use them to identify you.
  • Cloudflare: hosts this website and a small file the app reads when it opens, to know whether an update is required. Cloudflare then sees your device's IP address.
  • Apple: delivers notifications and processes in-app purchases.
  • PandaScore: provides schedules, scores, logos and photos. Logos and photos are downloaded directly from its servers, which therefore see your device's IP address. No information about your favorites is sent to it.
  • Twitch, YouTube and Kick: on a live match page, the video player of the platform streaming it is loaded only if you tap “Play the stream here”. That platform then receives technical data (IP address, device type), may set cookies and measure the audience of the video, and may show its own ads in the stream. It applies its own privacy policy. These cookies are kept in memory only and erased when the app is closed. YouTube is loaded in its privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com). Before that, the app shows the stream image that Twitch or YouTube publishes (a plain image, reloaded every 5 minutes): to download it, the platform receives your IP address, but no cookie is set or sent. It is not shown if you chose to hide scores.
  • Team news: when you open the news for a team you follow, the app reads public feeds directly from your device: Google News, YouTube (the feed of the team's channel videos), specialist press (Esports Insider, Dexerto, GamesIndustry, HLTV, Esports News UK, Eclypsia) and the team's own website (its RSS feed). Each of these services then receives your device's IP address and the request (for example the team name), and applies its own privacy policy. No information about your other favorites is sent to them.
  • CitoAPI: provides League of Legends player and team statistics. The app reads them through our server, which holds the key: CitoAPI does not see your device, only our server's requests.

Some of these providers are based in the United States. When data is transferred there, it is under the safeguards provided by the GDPR (adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses).

5. Advertising

The app shows no ads and contains no advertising tools: it does not use your device's advertising identifier and never asks for tracking permission. It is funded by the optional eSportly+ subscription.

6. eSportly+ subscription

The eSportly+ subscription is sold and charged by Apple through the App Store. We receive no payment information, name or email address. The app checks with Apple, on your device, whether you are subscribed. “Restore purchases” brings it back on your other devices using the same Apple account. You manage and cancel it in your Apple Account settings.

Apple informs our server of subscription events (subscription, trial, renewal, cancellation, refund). We keep only the event type, the plan, the date and a hashed fingerprint of the transaction number, which cannot be traced back to you: enough to count subscribers, nothing more. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in monitoring the service (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). Retention: 3 years at most.

6a. Technical report

iOS provides the app with reports about its crashes, hangs and slowdowns (Apple's MetricKit). They stay on your device for 14 days at most and contain neither your identity nor your favorites: technical data only. They are sent to us only if you choose to, from “About”, “Send the technical report”: you then decide to whom and how (email, message…). Legal basis: your choice to send them; we keep them while fixing the problem, 6 months at most.

6c. Website audience measurement

To know which pages of this website are read, each page loads an invisible image from our server (Supabase, European Union). We only count page views per day and per page. No cookies, no identifiers, and we keep neither your IP address, device nor referrer: there is no way to know who read what. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in improving the website (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)); this strictly anonymous measurement needs no consent. Counters are kept for 25 months at most.

Detailed audience measurement (Google Analytics): only if you accept it in the banner shown on your first visit. In that case, Google Analytics 4 (Google Ireland Limited, with possible transfers to the United States covered by the GDPR) sets two cookies (“_ga” and “_ga_…”, 13 months at most) and receives technical data (page views, device, browser, approximate country) that tell us which pages are read and where visits come from. We turn off Google signals and personalised advertising, and the data is kept for 14 months at most. Legal basis: your consent (GDPR, Article 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time with “Cookie settings” at the bottom of every page. Your choice (accept or refuse) is kept for 6 months in your browser (local storage, key “esportly-consent”), then we ask again. If you refuse, or until you answer, nothing is sent to Google and the site works exactly the same.

Without your consent, this website sets no cookies. If you choose the light or dark theme, that choice is kept in your browser (local storage, key “esportly-theme”) so it applies to the next pages. It never leaves your device, we never receive it, and it is removed if you switch back to “System theme” or clear the site’s data. This storage only serves what you asked for, so it needs no consent (ePrivacy Directive, Article 5(3)).

6d. A team's calendar

The “Add to my calendar” button on team, game and event pages subscribes your Calendar app to a public address, the same for everyone. We keep nothing about you: no identifier, no list of subscribers. As for any page, the host (Supabase, European Union) sees the IP address of the device that refreshes the calendar, in its technical logs kept for 7 days at most.

6e. Videos on the Blog

On the Blog, the page about each game may show videos from that game's official YouTube channels, as cards with a play button. Opening the page sends no request to YouTube or Google: a card is part of this website and loads nothing from YouTube. These cards appear only on the game pages of the Blog: the other pages of this website contain none.

When you click a card, the YouTube player loads in its place, in privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com), and the video starts. YouTube (Google) then receives your IP address and technical information from your browser, and may set cookies under its own privacy policy. We receive nothing from this exchange and send nothing else to YouTube. Without a click, nothing is loaded from YouTube. If JavaScript is turned off, or if you open a card in a new tab, the card simply opens the video's page on youtube.com.

6b. "Notify me at launch" (website)

On the esportly.eu website, the "Notify me at launch" form lets you receive a single email on launch day. We keep your email address and chosen language, sent by our server to Brevo to deliver the message. An unconfirmed address is deleted after 7 days; a confirmed address, after 12 months at most. You unsubscribe in one click from each email. Legal basis: your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time. The app itself still collects neither name nor email address.

7. What we do not do

  • In the app: no account, no sign-up, no collection of names or email addresses.
  • No advertising tracking tools and no third-party audience measurement: our only usage statistics are those in section 3b (which you can turn off), no technical report is sent without your choice, and on this website a simple page-view counter, without cookies, plus Google Analytics if you accept it (section 6c).
  • No selling or renting of data.

8. Minors

The app may be used by minors. It does not ask them for any personal information.

9. Your rights

You have the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict, object to and port your data, and you can withdraw your consent at any time.

  • Erasing your data from our server: turn off all alerts in the Settings tab (called “Alerts” in version 1.0), or stop following every team and competition. Deletion is immediate.
  • Any other request: write to contact@protojo.fr. We reply within one month. Because the app works without an account, we cannot find your data from your name or email address: tell us the “Support ID” shown in the Settings tab (called “Alerts” in version 1.0), under “About”.

If you are not satisfied with the answer, you can lodge a complaint with the French data protection authority, the CNIL.
